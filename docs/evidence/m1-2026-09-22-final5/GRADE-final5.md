## 6. Final assessment

What final5 proves about "every interaction pattern gets logged properly": the capture is lossless for what it retained (533,846 rows across 25 pages, per-page sequences contiguous, 0 detached dispatches) and, for the first time, records execution — 266,257 `function.enter` rows under 192 of 200 dispatched actions — so the ledger's claims can be checked against what ran rather than against fiber props alone. Every first-party request that reached the ledger is stamped with its causing action, 942 of 955 server spans join by exact trace id and W3C parent, all 3,768 db spans walk to a server span, and every Mongo write for the user in the window (34 timestamped rows across sessions, users, transactions, agents, aclentries, conversations, messages, sharedlinks, plus 23 session rotations and the six-collection delete cascade) is attributed to its own action with no bystander write and no silent write. All eight gate flows now reach a real write through that join, including the two final3 failures: agent-create produces `POST /api/agents` → `agents.insert` matching the Mongo row to the millisecond, and agent-chat goes to `POST /api/agents/chat/agents`. The final3 ledger defects are closed as specified: 0 cross-attributed requests (was 28), 0 outcomes without a dispatch, 35/35 correct stops with a mutation receipt, 120/120 unreachable reasons naming their own state, and 0 of 299 `not_encountered` units contradicted by execution. What remains unmeasured: whether each static unit was rendered (the bundle publishes only aggregate fiber counts, so 299 of 343 units have no render evidence either way, and the two screenshot contradictions — `DuringRunAction`, `ConvoLink` — show the fiber match still misses rendered elements); the DOM effect of anything in the scripted flow (mutation observation ran only for 43 state-local actions, leaving five `no_effect_witnessed` verdicts unfalsifiable); the content of the feedback, rename and edit writes (deleted by the run's own delete step before grading); and the 15 late-boot requests lost after the pre-navigation snapshot on the two sub-second pages. The biggest remaining cause, with evidence, is that **unit identity does not cover the handlers that execute**: the send, feedback, regenerate, agent-create and agent-chat handlers that the run proves ran (`ChatForm.tsx:17345:18011`, `Feedback.tsx:3347:3559`, `ContentRender.tsx:3465:3490`, `AgentPanel.tsx:13140:14819`) are not static units — no static handler is entered under any of those five actions — so 8 of 14 write-bearing actions credit nothing; the runtime-only units that do carry those identities are prop-site ids denied execution credit by `LIMITS.md`, which is why 73 of 111 runtime-only "unreachable" units have `function.enter` rows and the regenerate handler is ledgered "covered at seed-share-dialog" after reaching the DB twice; and 6 of the 57 credited chains are prop-match credits whose handler never ran under the action. Until execution is the primary key for every unit kind and the inventory includes the non-literal handlers (`handleSubmit(...)`, hook-returned callbacks) that drive the core patterns, 266k execution rows convert into only 6 execution-credited units. Honest coverage fraction: of the static units the evidence shows were rendered on visited states — 35 driven plus 9 found-but-unreachable, **44** (a floor; the remaining 299 have no render evidence) — **24 were driven to a positively verified outcome** (6 reached_db, 3 reached_backend, 15 correct_stop with the unit's own handler executed and a receipt), i.e. **55%**; 32 (73%) if the 8 `no_effect_witnessed` stops are counted as driven; 22 (50%) if the two bystander `reached_db` credits are excluded. Against the whole static inventory, that is 24 of 343 (7.0%) verified and 32 of 343 (9.3%) driven.
